## Overview

Two-factor authentication adds an extra layer of protection to your Dropbox account. After you enter your password, Dropbox asks for a second code from your phone. This step keeps your files safe even if someone learns your password.

You can turn on 2FA from your account settings in minutes. Dropbox supports authenticator apps and security keys so you can choose what works best for you.

<Callout kind="info">
  Enable 2FA today to reduce the chance of unauthorized access.
</Callout>

## Account Security

Start by signing in and opening your security settings. Look for the two-step verification option and follow the prompts to connect an authenticator app.

Dropbox sends a verification code during setup so you can confirm everything works before you finish.

## Device Management

Review the devices that have access to your account on a regular basis. Remove any device you no longer use.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Mobile" icon="smartphone">
    Open the Dropbox app, go to Settings, then Security. Tap Manage devices to see and remove phones or tablets.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Desktop" icon="monitor">
    Sign in at dropbox.com, click your avatar, choose Settings, then Security. Scroll to Devices and sign out from any computer you do not recognize.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Password Best Practices

Use a unique password for Dropbox that you do not reuse elsewhere. A strong password contains at least twelve characters with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols.

<Expandable title="How often should I change my password?" default-open="false">
  Change your password right away if you notice any unusual activity. Otherwise, update it every six to twelve months as a routine check.
</Expandable>

## Best Practices

- Store backup codes in a safe place so you can still sign in if you lose your phone.
- Turn on 2FA for every account that offers it, not only Dropbox.
- Check your security settings monthly to catch anything unusual early.
