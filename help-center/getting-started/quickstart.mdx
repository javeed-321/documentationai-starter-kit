## Get set up fast

If you need to get Zendesk ready for your team, this guide walks you through the fastest path: create your account, connect your first support channels, invite teammates, and test a basic ticket flow.

## Before you start

Make sure you have:

- An active Zendesk account
- Access to the email address and phone number you want to use for support
- Permission to add teammates and change channel settings
- A clear list of the first channels you want to support

<Callout kind="tip">

Start with one email channel first. Once that flow works, add chat, voice, or other channels one at a time.

</Callout>

## 1. Set up your account

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm your workspace details" icon="settings">
    Check your company name, time zone, and default support email in the account settings. These values affect ticket timestamps, notifications, and the sender name customers see.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Set up your support email" icon="mail">
    Add the email address your customers will use to reach support. Zendesk uses this address to create tickets from incoming messages.

    | Setting | What to use | Why it matters |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Support email | `support@yourcompany.com` | Customers send requests here |
    | Time zone | Your main support region | Keeps ticket timing accurate |
    | From name | Your team or brand name | Helps customers recognize replies |

  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose your first ticket form" icon="clipboard-list">
    Pick the fields you need on day one, such as subject, description, and priority. Keep it simple so agents can start working tickets right away.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## 2. Connect your first support channels

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect email" icon="mail">
    Add the mailbox you want to use for support and confirm message forwarding or routing, depending on how your account is set up. Send a test email to verify that it becomes a ticket.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Add chat or messaging" icon="message-circle">
    Connect your chat or messaging channel if you want customers to contact you in real time. Make sure you assign an owner for the channel and test the first incoming conversation.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Set up voice, if needed" icon="phone">
    Add your phone support channel and confirm call routing, business hours, and voicemail behavior before you announce the number to customers.

  </Step>
</Steps>

### Channel setup checklist

| Channel | What to confirm | Good first test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email | Forwarding, sender name, and ticket creation | Send a message from an outside inbox |
| Chat | Availability, greeting, and routing | Start a test conversation |
| Voice | Number, hours, and call handling | Place a test call |

<Callout kind="info">

If you connect more than one channel, test each one separately so you can spot routing or notification issues early.

</Callout>

## 3. Invite and onboard your team

<Steps>
  <Step title="Invite agents and admins" icon="users">
    Add the people who will manage tickets, configure settings, and respond to customers. Assign the right role for each person so access stays limited to what they need.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Set team responsibilities" icon="check-circle">
    Decide who handles triage, who owns escalations, and who reviews ticket quality. Write this down before you start taking live requests.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Show the basics" icon="book-open">
    Walk your team through the ticket list, ticket sidebar, comments, assignments, and status changes. Keep the first training focused on the most common actions.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## 4. Create a basic ticket flow

A simple flow helps your team work consistently from the start.

| Ticket step | What happens | Who owns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New | A customer sends a request | Zendesk creates the ticket |
| Open | An agent reviews and starts work | Assigned agent |
| Pending | You are waiting on the customer | Assigned agent |
| Solved | The issue is resolved | Assigned agent or admin |

### Suggested first workflow

1. A customer sends an email.
2. Zendesk creates a ticket automatically.
3. An agent gets assigned the ticket.
4. The agent replies and changes the status as work moves forward.
5. The ticket is marked solved when the issue is finished.

<Callout kind="tip">

Use one priority rule at first. For example, mark billing or outage tickets as high priority and leave everything else at normal priority.

</Callout>

## Common questions

<Expandable title="How do I know my email channel is working?" default-open="false">
  Send a test message from an external email address. If Zendesk creates a ticket and your team can reply from the ticket, the channel is working.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="How many channels should I connect first?" default-open="false">
  Start with one primary channel, then add the next channel after your team can reliably handle the first one. This keeps setup and training easier.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="What should I train agents on first?" default-open="false">
  Start with ticket status changes, assignment, public and private comments, and how to close a ticket. Those are the actions your team will use most often.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="What if tickets are not routing correctly?" default-open="false">
  Check your channel settings, assignment rules, and team permissions. Then send a fresh test ticket so you can trace the full path from intake to assignment.

</Expandable>

## Next steps

Once your first channels are live, you can add automation, reporting, routing rules, and more advanced team workflows.

## Still stuck?

If you cannot complete setup or your first channel does not create tickets correctly, contact Zendesk support for help.
