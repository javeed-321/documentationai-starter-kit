## Quickstart overview

Notion is an AI-powered workspace that helps you keep notes, docs, projects, calendar work, and email in one place. In this quickstart, you create a workspace, turn on AI features, and build a simple setup you can use right away.

If you are evaluating Notion for a team, focus on four outcomes: one shared workspace, one clear home for knowledge, one project workflow, and one connected view of meetings and messages. That is enough to get value quickly without adding extra structure too early.

<Callout kind="info">
This page is a starter kit. Replace the sample page names, team roles, and workflows with your own setup after you finish the first pass.
</Callout>

## Before you start

Make sure you have a Notion account and a clear first use case. For most new users, the fastest path is to create one workspace for your team or personal work, then add one docs area, one project area, and one place for AI-assisted notes.

You also get the smoothest onboarding if you decide in advance who owns setup, who can create pages, and which external tools you want to connect first. Keep the first version small so you can learn the product flow before scaling it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create your workspace" icon="users">
    Sign in to Notion and create a workspace for your team or personal use.

    Choose a workspace name that matches the way you plan to use it, such as a company name, team name, or personal workspace. Then invite the first people who need access.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on AI features" icon="zap">
    Open the AI area in Notion and enable the features you want to use first.

    Start with writing help, meeting notes, and search. If you plan to automate work routing, also set up agents so they can handle repetitive tasks and answer common questions.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Build your first pages" icon="book-open">
    Create three starter pages: one document, one wiki, and one project space.

    Use the document page for shared writing, the wiki for stable knowledge, and the project space for active work that changes often.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect your calendar and email" icon="calendar">
    Link your calendar and email so your workspace can support meetings and follow-ups in one place.

    This gives you a better starting point for planning, capturing meeting notes, and keeping work tied to the right conversations.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Minimal setup example

Use this simple structure for your first workspace. It gives you a clean foundation for docs, wikis, projects, and AI-assisted work without overbuilding.

```text
Workspace
├── Home
├── Docs
│   └── Team brief
├── Wiki
│   └── Policies and setup notes
├── Projects
│   └── Launch plan
└── AI
    ├── Meeting notes
    └── Search and agents
```

## What to build first

Start with docs, wikis, and projects because they give you fast, visible value. Docs help you create simple, readable pages for plans and references. Wikis help you centralize durable knowledge. Projects help you track work from idea to completion.

After that, add AI meeting notes and enterprise search so people can find information without hunting across pages. If your team handles recurring requests, create an agent that can route work or answer common questions before someone has to do it manually.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Docs" icon="book-open">
    Use docs for planning pages, meeting notes, and short working drafts.

    Keep each doc focused on one topic so people can scan it quickly and link it from other pages.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Wikis" icon="database">
    Use wikis for policies, onboarding, reference material, and other knowledge that should stay stable.

    Organize pages by topic and keep the top level easy to browse.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Projects" icon="rocket">
    Use projects for active work with owners, status, and deadlines.

    Add only the fields you need at first, then expand the structure as your process becomes clearer.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## How to use AI well

AI in Notion is most useful when it works on top of good structure. Give it clear pages, clear names, and clear ownership so it can summarize, draft, and route work accurately.

A good first setup is to use AI for meeting notes, writing assistance, and search. Once those are reliable, introduce agents that can automate repetitive work and help keep information moving across the workspace.

<Expandable title="Recommended first AI tasks" default-open="false">
  Use AI for these early wins:

  - Draft a page from rough notes
  - Summarize a meeting into action items
  - Find a policy or project detail quickly
  - Rewrite content for clarity
  - Route a request to the right team

</Expandable>
